# Creator Coin, in one page

*For brand managers and founders. No maths required.*

## Today, someone guesses

Every campaign gets a payout ladder set by feel: *10K views → ₹500, 50K → ₹2,000*. In our replay of
120 past campaigns:

- **22 went over budget.** The worst by 6×.
- **Small creators got paid 19% of the time.** Big ones, 81%. One ladder can't fit a 5K page and a 5M page.
- **₹4.1 lakh went to bought views.** Nobody was checking.

## What we do instead

**You answer four questions.** What you're promoting, where, how much, how long. That's the setup.

**Every real view becomes a coin.** Your budget backs all the coins. More views means each coin is
worth less, so you never pay more than your budget. It's arithmetic, not a forecast.

**The market sets the price.** When the campaign ends, your budget is split across every coin. If too
few views came in, paying the whole budget would overpay, so we settle at a fair price and **give you
the rest back**.

**Creators get goals made for them.** Their first goal is what 4 in 5 creators their size actually
hit. Each goal after that is a real stretch. A post is paid for the highest goal it reaches, and every
creator gets the same price per coin. Views between goals are counted in the price but not paid; that
money comes back to you.

**Slow week for everyone? Goals come down.** We check at the quarter marks. If a whole group of
creators can't reach their goals, the goals are lowered so they stay reachable. We call it Fair Reach.

**Bought views don't get paid.** Real views fade slowly. Bought ones arrive in a burst and stop dead.
We hold posts that fall off a cliff, check them within 7 days, and never pay for fakes.

## Did it work? The same posts, paid both ways

| | The old way | Creator Coin |
|---|---:|---:|
| Campaigns over budget | 22 of 120 | **0** |
| Cost per 1,000 real views | ₹58 | **₹41** |
| Small creators paid | 19% | **79%** |
| Paid for bought views | ₹4.1 lakh | **₹0.9 lakh** |
| Price swing between similar campaigns | 0.67 | **0.44** |

## Where it can still get it wrong

- **We can't replay how creators react.** A campaign paying well should attract more creators. A
  replay of the same posts can't show that; the live simulator shows it as a scenario, not a promise.
- **Fakes that drip in slowly can slip through.** We only see view counts. Engagement or watch-time
  data would close the gap.
- **Creators don't know their exact pay until the end.** That's the cost of letting the market set it.
  They see the live value the whole way.
- **All of this runs on generated data.** It's made at run time by random processes nobody tuned, and
  you can change the recipe yourself on the Backtest page. The real test is running it alongside live
  campaigns.
