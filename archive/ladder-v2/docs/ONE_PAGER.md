# Creator Pool, in one page

*For brand managers and founders. No code required.*

## The problem today

Ops sets every milestone ladder by feel: *10K views → ₹500, 50K → ₹2,000…*. In our replay of 25 past
campaigns:

- **4 of 25 went over budget**, one by 2.6×.
- Small creators got paid **21%** of the time, big ones **100%**.
- **46%** of the views on paid posts earned nothing, because a ladder rounds every post down to its last rung.
- Nobody can say what a view is actually worth. If the brand names a price, it can name a low one.

## What the new system does

**1. You set a budget, and pick who and what it's for.**
Choose any number of categories, platforms and creator sizes, or none to leave it open to all. There is
no rate for you to lowball and no guaranteed minimum for creators to farm.

**2. Rungs come from how far posts like yours actually travel.**
Each creator size and format gets its own rungs. The first rung, the minimum, is reached by 4 in 5
posts like theirs; each next rung by half of those on the one before. So a small meme page and a
million-follower page face the same odds.

**3. Real views become coins.**
Two ways to pay, both shown side by side:
- **Coin rungs**, the ladder the brief asks for: a post earns the coins of the highest rung it reached.
- **No rungs**: past the minimum, every view is one coin.

Your budget backs all the coins, so the more views arrive, the less each coin is worth.

**4. The price is found when the campaign ends.**
- **Plenty of views:** your whole budget is split across every coin. You bought all that reach
  for exactly your budget.
- **Too few views:** paying the whole budget for them would be far above what views cost elsewhere
  on the platform. So the price is split fairly between your budget and that market rate, and **you
  get the rest back**.

**5. Your budget can never be exceeded.** Not in 95 of 100 cases. In every case.

**6. Bought views don't get paid.** A suspicious post earns nothing until it is checked.

**7. You can play the campaign out before launch.** Pick a scenario (normal, too many join, too few
join, a last-day viral post, a fraud wave) and see day by day what the coin is worth, how it settles,
and what every creator is paid. Each run has a seed, so it can be replayed exactly.

## Did it work? The same real posts, replayed

| | Today | Coin rungs | No rungs |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Campaigns within budget | 21 of 25 | **25 of 25** | **25 of 25** |
| Worst overspend | 164% over | **none** | **none** |
| Creators paid anything | 53% | 79% | 79% |
| Small vs big creators paid | 21% vs 100% | 75% vs 90% | 75% vs 90% |
| Views on paid posts that earned nothing | 46% | 34% | **0%** |

Creators earn more per view than today's ladders paid (median ₹71–73 vs ₹35 per 1,000). That is
because today's ladders often paid out a fraction of the budget; Creator Pool pays out what you
committed whenever the reach is there, and refunds when it isn't.

## Where it can still get it wrong

- **We can't replay how creators react.** A campaign paying above market should attract more creators
  and bring its price down. Past posts can't show that; the simulator shows scenarios, not a forecast.
- **Creators don't know their exact pay until the end.** That is the cost of letting the market set it.
  They see the live value throughout.
- **New platforms or categories** start with little history, so rungs and the market rate are broader at first.
- **Smart fraud** that imitates natural growth can slip through; engagement data would close the gap.
- **This was tested on realistic but invented data.** The next step is running it alongside live campaigns.
