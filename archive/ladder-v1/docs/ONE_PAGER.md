# Milestone ladders, in one page

*For brand managers and founders. No code required.*

## The problem today

Every campaign pays creators through a ladder of view milestones: *10K views → ₹500, 50K →
₹2,000…*. Ops sets these by feel. In our replay of 23 past campaigns:

- **6 of 23 went over budget**, one by 3×.
- Small creators hit a milestone **40%** of the time, big ones **95%**. The same ladder is out of
  reach for one and a free lunch for the other.
- Nobody can say why a milestone is 250K and not 200K.

## What the new method does

**1. It learns how far content really travels.**
It looks at past posts like yours (same category, platform, creator size and format) and learns what
a typical post gets and how widely results vary. Gaming reels travel further than finance carousels,
and the ladder knows it.

**2. It places each milestone by the odds of reaching it.**
- The first milestone is what a *typical* post for that creator size achieves.
- Each next one is reached by *half* of the posts that made the previous one.
- So every creator size faces the same odds. Small pages get reachable targets; big pages don't get
  easy money.
- "Why 250K?" now has an answer: *1 in 8 posts like this get there.*

**3. Every view pays the same rate.**
- You set the most you'll pay per 1,000 views. That is a hard ceiling, so you never pay more for
  reach than you said it's worth.
- A creator who reaches a milestone is paid for that milestone's views. 141K views on a 140K
  milestone pays for 140K.

**4. Your budget holds, in 95 of 100 scenarios.**
- Before launch, the system simulates your campaign 3,000 times and sets a **guaranteed minimum rate**
  that the budget can pay even when views come in high.
- Creators see both numbers: *"reach 57K → at least ₹1,359, up to ₹2,280."*
- While the campaign runs, it keeps a tally of what's been promised. If a burst of viral posts would
  exceed the budget, it stops accepting new posts instead of overspending.

**5. You get back what isn't earned.**
- At the end, creators are paid the highest rate the budget allows, up to your ceiling.
- Whatever is left is **refunded** to you.

**6. Bought views don't get paid.**
- Nothing is paid until a post's growth has been checked.
- Bought views arrive in a burst and then stop; real ones keep growing.
- Posts that look bought are held, and aren't paid if confirmed.

## Did it work? The same real posts, replayed under both ladders

| | Today | New method |
|---|---:|---:|
| Campaigns within budget | 17 of 23 | **23 of 23** |
| Worst overspend | 200% over | **none** |
| Paid to creators who bought views | ₹1,84,700 | **₹3,522** |
| Milestone hit rate, small vs big creators | 40% vs 95% | **59% vs 60%** |
| Creators hitting any milestone | 62% | 57% |

The hit rate falls slightly overall, because big creators no longer clear milestones that were
trivially easy for them.

## Where it can still get it wrong

- **We can't yet predict how creators will react.** The replay assumes the same people post the
  same content. A fairer ladder may attract more small creators, or fewer big ones. Only real
  campaigns can tell us.
- **Small budgets for big reach.** If far more creators join than the budget can pay at your rate,
  everyone is paid less per view, down to the guaranteed minimum, or the campaign fills up early.
  The system tells you this before launch.
- **Brand-new kinds of campaign.** With little history, the system borrows from the closest similar
  campaigns and labels the ladder as a broader estimate.
- **Very viral posts.** The highest milestones are reached a bit more often than forecast, because
  real virality is wilder than the model.
- **Smart fraud.** Bot views that imitate natural growth can slip through. Engagement data (likes,
  comments, audience location) would close that gap.
- **This was tested on realistic but invented data.** The next step is running it alongside the
  current process on live campaigns.
