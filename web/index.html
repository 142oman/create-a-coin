01The problem

An advertiser arrives with a budget and wants to advertise through creators.

To pay them, they must define a reward: this many views earns this much.

That number is arbitrary. They can't know how publishers will behave, or how audiences will respond to them.

So every reward ladder is a gamble.

Lose, and they overspend the budget. Set the price high for an easy target, and they overpay for every impression.

02What a solution looks like

Real ad markets don't guess prices. Bidders compete, and the market discovers the price.

Nobody sets it by hand. It's how advertising already works, at enormous scale.

price discovered
01

No bidding market.

Creator campaigns have no crowd of advertisers competing, so nothing reveals the price.

02

Sticky placement.

A live post can't be swapped for an advertiser who pays more. So we need a way to pay fairly for reach that has already happened, without breaking the budget.

03The solution

You give us two things: a budget, and a rough price per impression (CPI) you'd be happy with.

That's all. Then we do our thing.

Under the hood

Market formation, a second pegged liquid commodity, and inflation minting, working together to find the true price of an impression.

01

Market formation

A locked pool of value meets a growing supply of impressions. Where they meet, a market forms. Its clearing price is the price.

02

Inflation minting

Verified impressions are minted as they happen. Supply grows only with real reach, and each new unit dilutes the price.

03

Pegged liquid commodity

A second commodity, pegged to your rough CPI, stays liquid against the pool. It anchors the price while supply is thin.

04

Continuous clearing

The price is recomputed as supply changes, and settles when the campaign ends.

04The result

We always find the true price of an impression.

The price comes from real reach, not from a guess.

Advertisers always pay the fair price. Publishers are always paid the fair price.

05What that unlocks

A rung is just a target. You never have to guess its price. The mechanism works it out.

Building rungs stops being a financial decision. It becomes a UX and gamification problem.
