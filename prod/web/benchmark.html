How a campaign is simulated
1 · An advertiser shows up

He has a budget (₹20K–₹2 Cr) and a gut feel for price per view. 100 means he's got it right; higher or lower means he's off.

2 · He builds a ladder, by gut

One ladder, same 8 rungs for every creator size, priced so that — if his feel were right — it would spend exactly the budget.

3 · A market shows up

Creators of every size post. Sometimes a crowd floods in, sometimes too few arrive, sometimes a post goes viral.

4 · Both get paid, and we compare

The old ladder pays whatever crosses a rung — no ceiling. Clearing gets the same budget and feel, builds its own rungs per size from past data, and never pays past the budget.

Unbiased mode (the switch above) swaps every fixed number below for a wide random draw — its banner lists exactly what.

The exact numbers
  1. Budget is drawn between ₹20,000 and ₹2 crore (log-uniform).
  2. CPI feel (5 to 500, 100 = the market's true CPI). Above 100 he expects more people than the budget buys, below 100 fewer. His raw number goes straight to Clearing as its reference price — unmodified, exactly as the real app does.
  3. Clearing's price is engine.settle, the same function the live app calls. If enough views arrive that the budget would split to a price at or under his reference, that lower, liquidity-set price wins and his number is ignored (the "plenty" regime). If views are thin and the budget would split to a price above his reference, the price converges toward a negotiated point between the two — the geometric mean of the pool price and his reference (the "thin" regime). A wrong reference only ever matters in a thin market, and then it's pulled toward reality, not clipped by anything I added.
  4. Rungs, old way. Eight rungs, one ladder for every creator. Each has a score (5 to 300) for where he thinks 80% will cross: 100 is right, more is too high, less too low. The scores share a campaign-wide bias and a tilt, on top of his feel. Payouts are sized so a market that behaves as he pictures spends exactly the budget.
  5. Creators come in four sizes (nano, micro, mid, macro), about 1,000× apart in views, with lognormal views inside each.
  6. Market. Smooth, abundant (a crowd floods in), scarce (too few arrive), one viral surge, or many. A surge lifts a random share of one size group's posts at once; a few single posts also spike. His rungs nudge the crowd only slightly.
  7. Clearing builds rungs per size from 300 past posts of that size, runs one Fair Reach check per size, and prices with the engine's own settle. The engine is read, never changed, so other pages are unaffected.
  8. Overspent = paid more than the budget, by any amount. Overpriced = within budget, but the money paid past a "fair cost" (1.5× the true price for the reach delivered) is itself more than half the budget — a real, large overpay, not just a high price on a small spend. Paid at or under the true price = the price actually paid per view was at or below it. The true price is only a guide; with one seller and many buyers, overpaying is the real risk.

No fraud check, by design. Fair Reach runs once on final views instead of at 25/50/75%. Creators are paid on final views only. Every number here comes from assumptions in ladder/benchmark.py, not from measured data.
